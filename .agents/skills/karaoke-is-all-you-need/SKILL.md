# Karaoke Is All You Need

Use the scripts, file formats and commands in the [project README](https://github.com/holmesworcester/karaoke-is-all-you-need#readme). If the skill was installed separately from the tools, locate or clone [the repository](https://github.com/holmesworcester/karaoke-is-all-you-need) and run its commands from the checkout. Work on each song in its own directory.

1. **Identify the recording.** Match the requested release, duration, arrangement and first vocal entrance. Listen to candidate instrumentals for watermarks, cues, alternate edits and drift. When an instrumental is unsuitable, run `scripts/separate.py` on the chosen master. Keep the vocal estimate for alignment and the stereo instrumental for singing.
   For the live karaoke desk, deliver both playback mixes for every song and cut. **Full** (`backing.wav`) contains the complete accompaniment with vocals removed. **Stripped** (`drums.wav`) contains just the isolated percussion. When a drum stem is missing, run `scripts/separate_drums.py backing.wav DRUM_MODEL.onnx drums.wav` with the HT-Demucs FT drums specialist. Keep the isolated vocal in `guide.wav` for timing and test singing. Apply identical offsets, tempo changes, repeats and cut boundaries to all three stems. Audition both playback mixes at the same sung entrance and loop seam and verify their lengths before publishing.
   For the live desk, choose Teaser and Main independently on the exact playback edition. Teaser features one recognizable opening line or short clause, followed by a clear accompaniment-only gap before the repeat. Main features the full chorus or principal refrain. Find beat one from the drum pattern and harmonic changes at both ends. A sung pickup can fall before beat one, and a held ending can cross it. Include the extra bar when needed. Set the vocal gate in the rests around the intended words. Check the first consonant, the last release and the words on the singer display over two passes, with both full backing and percussion. Before choosing an instrumental riff, check the song’s other editions for a sung hook. A guitar part and a timed chorus can share one library title. Assign the song once from the operator’s library. Use the mic pane’s T, M and F buttons to verify the Teaser, Main and Full editions on the singer’s screen. Each recording has a single library row. For a wholly instrumental song, choose a recognizable riff and its longer return.
2. **Prepare the transcript.** Use a line-timed LRC for the same edition. Check repeated hooks, contractions, ad-libs and empty instrumental rows against the voice. Record the lyric edition and the audio source. Use `scripts/align.py` on the reference vocal to obtain a first pass. Request `--raw-output` to retain confidence and alignment evidence. The bundled model works with English words.
   Audit for omitted words before final alignment. Transcribe the exact recording or a vocal stem independently of the lyric sheet. Compare the two on the recording clock, including phrase endings, unscored passages, ad-libs, transitions and short retriggers. `scripts/audit_omissions.py SCORE.json OBSERVATIONS.json REPORT.json` produces candidates. Confirm each addition against the mix and a second observation such as a different stem, matching vocal sample or phoneme attack. Record the heard words, attack and release. A long speech-recognition segment in a musical rest or a phrase copied from a different edit needs local acoustic evidence.
3. **Review the timing by ear.** Play the vocal and watch the sweep through the first entrance, fast lines, repeats and final entrance. Use the model scores to select additional passages for inspection. Compare a second vocal estimate or cross-correlate recurring snippets if a line lands on an echo or jumps a repetition. Fix the affected word intervals against the audio clock. Verify the beginning, middle and end of an externally sourced backing to distinguish offset from tempo drift. The [demo analysis](../../../demo/lights-burn-dimmer/analysis/README.md) illustrates repairs specific to one recording.
   Check both ends of each cue. Mark a start at the first identifiable lead consonant or vowel. Mark the end where the sung vowel or final consonant releases. A speech recognizer can finish spelling a word while the singer is still holding it. Run `scripts/refine_releases.py VOCAL.wav timing.json releases.json --output proposed.json` on the isolated vocal. Inspect the sensitivity range, long holds, overlapping harmonies, and proposals that reach the following word. A repeated vowel or a reverb tail can mimic a continuous hold. Compare spectrograms and the source mix when the stem is ambiguous. For suspected global lateness, mark a sample of clear attacks across the recording without displaying the current cues, check the signed offsets, and repair isolated outliers separately. Verify the sweep remains in progress mid-hold, finishes at release, and lines up after a seek in each export.
   For a DJ set, fingerprint short sections against the specific released mixes. Fit local tempo and offset across multiple independent landmarks. Keep loops and overlapping transitions on the performance clock, and compare candidate releases against each other before listing two identifications of the same sample. A studio lyric sheet may omit bars or label a rearranged hook incorrectly. Check repeated phrases on the separated remix before transferring both endpoints. For a chopped synth lead, estimate the phonemes on the isolated recording. Check envelope valleys for repeated attacks on the same vowel and keep a continuous hold across pitch changes when the vowel has no new attack. Time the lyric and MIDI events independently on the same clock. Inspect the transition before the first confident fingerprint, then listen through the end for processed speech. Confirm the spoken cues appear in the rendered video. Check the separation in both stereo channels and compare the drum and bass transients against the source. When a clean backing phrase repeats, match its harmony and beat phase before using it to replace a contaminated passage.
   In a set-wide release audit, scan every cue on its own vocal lane. Listen to the first consonant of the following word as well as the preceding vowel. A wrong following onset can cap the vowel estimate. Compare short and sustained takes of the same hook independently. In a DJ loop, measure successive attacks on the performance and check the tail of each sample. The DJ can retrigger two words from a longer phrase under the next song. Keep a record of ambiguous chops and their syllable timing.
   For overlapping voices, align each part on its own clock and give it a fixed display lane. Listen for short final words that forced alignment squeezed below the duration of one video frame. Measure the vowel attack and release on the recording, then check the encoded word at two positions within its hold. Use `scripts/check_sweep.py MOVIE SCORE.ass WORD START END --lane NAME --fonts assets` for outlined ASS lyrics with a lighter highlight. Add `--held` to check that the sweep continues past the midpoint of a sustained word.
4. **Build the requested presentation.** Run `scripts/art.py` with the title and cover, then `scripts/build-player.mjs` with the song manifest. Use the two lyric preview lanes. Export a standalone MP3+G (CD+G) archive only when the user explicitly requests that format. Mark a pickup row when a short pause makes the next entrance hard to hear. Give the native tile reveal and the palette ramp enough time to read. Use full-bleed artwork by default. For a cover with lettering that supplies its own title, use an artwork-only intro and preserve the lettering with framed artwork. Set the song’s fade duration to suit its instrumental lead-in and leave room for the first pickup. Use the lighter of the two lyric colors for the highlight sweep, including on light backgrounds. Compare relative luminance after CD+G palette quantization and check both colors against the background. On performance footage, outline both colors for visibility over changing scenes. For a clean instrumental under dialogue, match the independently released accompaniment to the live recording by multiple attacks, correct any clock drift, and place stereo crossfades in correlated, speech-free passages. When the user requests switchable subtitles on footage, a single video clock can drive the word sweep, Arabic shaping, palette and positions in `src/lyric-overlay.js`.
   For release cuts, identify the artist’s music video or official visualizer. Compare its soundtrack to the mastered recording across each edit. Use the selected release artwork wherever the video has no matching section. Keep the mix and all word times on the reviewed musical clock. Honor the singer’s choices about cues, graphics and format.
5. **Verify the deliverable.** Open the actual player. When a matching original is available, include it via `originalAudio` and use the audio switch to check the same lyric sweep in both modes. Seek while paused, switch during a word, and confirm both the position and the release hold. Seek both directions, pause mid-word, change speed, go fullscreen and play offline. Check the animated edge on a descender, a delayed pickup, the very last word and the artwork hold. When delivering a requested MP3+G archive, decode its graphics packets to verify the export. For MP4, run `video/export.mjs` and compare decoded frames and audio with independent seeks in the player. A browser sweep that matches its own timestamp table tests rendering; listening and waveform landmarks test the table against the singer.
6. **Hand off the requested files.** Give the user a direct playable file or link and identify the recording and lyric edition. Report concrete timing checks and passages that still need human judgment. Apply the visibility the user requested for the audio and demo.
